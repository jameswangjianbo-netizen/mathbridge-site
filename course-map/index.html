How the two years are built

The guide is not a teaching order. This course is sequenced so that every new idea has somewhere to land, and every big concept is met at least three times — informally, formally, then extended.

Five conceptual threads

Each thread is introduced early, revisited in a second context, then extended at AHL. When you plan a lesson, name the thread aloud — that is what turns content into concept.

Thread 01

Equivalence & form

The same object wearing different clothes: vertex vs factored quadratics, Cartesian vs polar complex numbers, partial fractions, trig identities, integration by rearrangement. Met W2 → W14 → DP2 W4.

Thread 02

Generalization & proof

Pattern → conjecture → justification → proof. Deductive proof in W2, induction on series in W6, De Moivre by induction in DP2 W6, Maclaurin as generalized approximation in DP2 W23. The spine of Paper 3.

Thread 03

Function as a lens

Domain, range, inverse and transformation established once in W10–13, then reused for every new family: exponential, polynomial, rational, circular, inverse trigonometric.

Thread 04

Change & accumulation

The derivative is met in DP1 (W19) so it can be used as a graphing tool for a whole year before integration arrives in DP2. Differentiation and integration are deliberately separated by nine months.

Thread 05

Uncertainty & inference

Deliberately taught late (DP2 W13–17) when students have integration for continuous random variables and combinatorics for the binomial — so probability is derived, not asserted.

Running

The toolkit

30 hours of inquiry, modelling and investigation are not a unit — they are the fourth block of selected weeks: Toolkit tasks, Paper 3 Labs, and the exploration itself.

Sequencing decisions and why

Proof comes first, not last

Most schemes park AHL 1.15 next to complex numbers. Here contradiction and counterexample are taught in week 2 and induction in week 3, because every subsequent unit then has a legitimate proof task available: sums of series (W6), De Moivre (DP2 W6), higher derivatives (W23), divisibility. Proof stops being a topic and becomes a habit.

Differentiation before trigonometry

Placing Calculus 1 (W19–23) before the trigonometric functions means that when SL 3.7 arrives, students already own the language of maxima, minima and concavity and can analyse a sin(b(x+c))+d rather than merely describe it. It also means the derivatives of sin and cos in W31 are an extension of a known process, not a new one.

Complex numbers after trigonometry

Modulus–argument form, Euler form and De Moivre are unteachable without fluent exact values and compound angle identities. Those are settled in DP1 W27–W30; complex numbers follow in DP2 W4–7, where De Moivre repays the trigonometry by generating identities in the other direction.

Statistics late, and integrated

Continuous random variables (AHL 4.14) are an integration topic wearing a statistics hat. Teaching probability after Calculus 3 lets students find modes, medians and variances of pdfs by integrating rather than by rule-following, and lets the binomial distribution be justified from the counting principles taught in DP2 W1–2.

On hours: 240 blocks, 240 hours

Four 85-minute blocks a week over 60 teaching weeks is about 340 contact hours. The IB recommends 240 hours for an HL course, of which 30 belong to the toolkit and the exploration. The surplus is not slack — it is the retrieval starters, the peer marking, the reteach clinics, the mock series and the revision programme, all of which are timetabled here rather than assumed. If your calendar gives you fewer weeks, cut from the revision weeks and the toolkit blocks before you cut from the content units.

The last five weeks are not new content

All syllabus content closes at DP2 week 23. Week 24 is mocks; weeks 25–27 are diagnosis-driven — clinics chosen from mock data, a no-GDC fluency programme, and Paper 3 under timing.

Formative assessment architecture

Four nested cycles: a hinge question and an exit ticket in every one of the 240 blocks, a retrieval starter every lesson, eighteen unit formatives, and seven cumulative retrieval quizzes. Nothing here is reported except the unit formatives and the two examination series.

Cycle 1 · every block

Hinge & exit

One hinge question at minute 45 with misconception-mapped distractors, one exit ticket at minute 80. Both are read before the next lesson is planned. No marks recorded.

Cycle 2 · every week

Retrieval starter

Five interleaved questions, self-marked in two minutes. Weekly totals go into the student’s own error log — not into the gradebook.

Cycle 3 · every unit

Unit formative + peer marking

A 40–50 minute paper under exam conditions, then peer-marked against the real IB markscheme. The marking is the learning. Best four per semester are reported.

Cycle 4 · seven times in two years

Cumulative retrieval quiz

Twenty questions spanning everything taught to date, GDC-free, at DP1 weeks 7, 13, 20 and 30 and DP2 weeks 7, 12 and 17. Tracked on a single running chart per student so decay is visible.

The exploration, milestone by milestone

Fifteen of the thirty toolkit hours belong to the exploration. They are distributed here rather than blocked, so investigation skills are built long before the IA is named.

DP1 W6 · Toolkit 1

Pattern to conjecture

A 45-minute mini-exploration on figurate numbers, written up in 300 words. Feedback uses criterion E language deliberately, without naming the IA.

DP1 W13 · Toolkit 2

The modelling cycle

Fit, test, reflect, adapt. Students model one real data set of their own choosing and critique the fit — the first rehearsal of criterion D.

DP1 W22 · Toolkit 3

Reading a published exploration

Two moderated exemplars (19 marks and 16 marks) marked blind by students against all five criteria, then compared with the official marks.

DP1 W28 · Launch

Exploration launched

Criteria unpacked, stimulus workshop, ethics and academic integrity including the IB position on AI tools. Students leave with three candidate areas of interest.

DP1 W31 · Deadline

Form B due — topic, rationale, aim

Signed by student and teacher and binding. One round of written feedback follows within two weeks. Contributes to the semester’s best-four formative grades.

DP1 summer

Data collection and reading

Students gather primary or secondary data, or work the mathematics of their investigation, and log sources as they go.

DP2 W1–3 · Clinics

Problem specification and scope

One-to-one consultations. The commonest fix at this stage is narrowing: a good exploration does a few things well.

DP2 W6 · Deadline

Full first draft

The only draft the teacher may read. Late submissions follow your school’s academic policy; no feedback is guaranteed.

DP2 W10

Written feedback returned

Advice on how the work could improve, criterion by criterion. Errors are indicated, never corrected. Feedback conversations are documented and acknowledged.

DP2 W12 · Deadline

Final exploration submitted

PDF, double-spaced, page-numbered, anonymised. Naming convention: Name_studentcode_AAHL_IA.pdf.

DP2 W14–16

Internal standardisation

Department cross-marking, mark entry, sample selection for moderation.

Paper 3 as a taught strand

Paper 3 is not tested by extra past papers alone — it is a distinct skill: sustained reasoning from a single theme towards a generalization. Seven labs run through DP2, each one block, each ending in a written generalization with justification — fortnightly through term 1, then roughly monthly once the papers get longer.

The deepest change is not content. The 2029 guide replaces the twelve fundamental concepts with a mathematics framework: an inquiry process of problem specification → abstraction → computation → interpretation, wrapped in four attributes of mathematical thinking (reasoning, communication, linking themes, dispositions). Each sub-topic carries a guiding question and linking questions instead of conceptual understandings. Practically: the F/C/D inquiry questions in this scheme map cleanly onto guiding and linking questions, and the exploration becomes a much tighter, more structured document.
