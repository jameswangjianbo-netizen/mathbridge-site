The lesson-plan library

Five big ideas. Three ways into each.

Each short outline names the learning move, a quick check for understanding, and evidence students can produce. Adjust the examples, timing and level to fit your class.

Pathway / 01

Number & proportion

Move from multiplicative thinking to rates and growth; make units and the meaning of a ratio explicit.

IGCSE → MYP

Ratios you can see

Use a recipe or map scale to compare equivalent ratios with tables, diagrams and unit rates.

Quick check

Which representation makes the scale factor easiest to spot? Explain.

Assessment evidence

Scale a new recipe and justify each conversion with units.

IGCSE → MYP

Rates tell a story

Connect distance-time tables, graphs and unit rates; compare journeys with different starting points.

Quick check

Two lines have different slopes. Which journey is faster, and what does the intercept mean?

Assessment evidence

Annotate a journey graph and support a comparison with calculations.

MYP → DP

Growth, repeated

Contrast repeated percentage change with constant additive change using a table and a spreadsheet or calculator.

Quick check

After several steps, why are the two patterns no longer close?

Assessment evidence

Choose and defend a model for a savings or depreciation scenario.

Pathway / 02

Algebra & functions

Connect symbols to structure, representations and solutions students can interpret.

IGCSE

Same value, new form

Use area models to expand and factorise expressions, then identify which form answers a given question.

Quick check

Which expression shows the roots most clearly? How do you know?

Assessment evidence

Match three expressions to diagrams and explain one match algebraically.

IGCSE → MYP

Two quantities, one rule

Build a linear model from a context; move between a verbal rule, table, graph and equation.

Quick check

What does the gradient mean here? What does the intercept mean?

Assessment evidence

Create a model from data and use it to answer an unfamiliar question.

MYP → DP

Quadratics, three views

Compare factorised, expanded and completed-square forms with the roots, intercept and vertex on a graph.

Quick check

Without plotting, which form gives the turning point most directly?

Assessment evidence

Choose an efficient form to solve a problem and interpret the result.

Pathway / 03

Geometry & trigonometry

Build from visual relationships to precise reasoning, choosing a representation that fits the problem.

IGCSE → MYP

Similarity and scale

Investigate how lengths, areas and volumes change when a shape is enlarged.

Quick check

If the side length doubles, what happens to the area? Show why.

Assessment evidence

Use a scale factor to find a missing measure and explain its dimension.

IGCSE

Choose a trig ratio

Label sides relative to an angle, then decide when sine, cosine or tangent is useful.

Quick check

Which two sides are known or needed? How does that guide your choice?

Assessment evidence

Solve a height or distance problem; include a labelled diagram.

MYP → DP

Angles around us

Model a periodic situation with a sketch, then discuss how amplitude and period change the model.

Quick check

Which feature of the context gives the period? How can the graph show it?

Assessment evidence

Fit a trigonometric model to a simple data set and critique its limits.

Pathway / 04

Statistics & probability

Make students' reasoning visible: ask what the data support, not only which calculation to perform.

IGCSE → MYP

One data set, two stories

Compare distributions with plots and summary statistics; notice how outliers affect the story.

Quick check

Would you describe the centre with a mean or median? Explain your choice.

Assessment evidence

Compare two groups and support a conclusion with a plot and statistic.

IGCSE → MYP

Are the events independent?

Use two-way tables, tree diagrams or simulation to distinguish independent from dependent events.

Quick check

Does learning the first outcome change the probability of the second?

Assessment evidence

Represent a two-stage situation and explain the assumptions in your model.

MYP → DP

From sample to claim

Explore how sample size and variation affect a claim; use repeated samples or a simulation.

Quick check

Would a different sample likely change the conclusion? Why?

Assessment evidence

Write a claim with supporting data and one limitation of the sample.

Pathway / 05

Change & calculus

Prepare for calculus by treating rate of change as an idea first, then formalising it with functions and notation.

IGCSE → MYP

Average rate of change

Compare intervals on a curve and connect average rate to the slope of a secant line.

Quick check

What changes when the interval becomes shorter?

Assessment evidence

Calculate and compare average rates over two intervals; interpret the units.

MYP → DP

Zooming in on a tangent

Estimate instantaneous rate by shrinking an interval and connecting the pattern to a tangent gradient.

Quick check

How can you tell your estimate is settling toward a value?

Assessment evidence

Show a table of estimates and explain what the limiting value represents.

DP AA

Derivative as a decision tool

Use a derivative to identify where a model increases, decreases or reaches a local optimum.

Quick check

Does the stationary point make sense in the original context?

Assessment evidence

Solve an optimisation problem and justify the answer in context.

Assessment that informs teaching

Look for thinking, not just finished answers.

Use a small, repeatable cycle to find out what students understand and decide what to do next.

A

Name the evidence

Write one observable success criterion tied to the lesson goal.

B

Check every learner

Use a hinge question, mini-whiteboard response or quick sort—not only volunteers.

C

Respond while it matters

Reteach a misconception, offer a scaffold or extend the reasoning based on the responses.

D

Ask for transfer

Finish with a new example that asks students to apply and explain the same idea.

Try this exit ticket

Quadratic functions

A student says the roots of a quadratic are the same as its turning point. Use a sketch or example to explain why that is not always true.

What to look for

Does the response connect roots to x-intercepts, identify the vertex separately, and support the explanation with a representation?

Use inquiry, multiple representations and explanation to make relationships explicit.

Generalise & justify

Extend ideas with abstraction, technology and increasingly independent mathematical reasoning.

Using MathBridge

Useful starting points for teachers.

Are these complete scripted lessons?

No. They are adaptable lesson outlines with a learning focus, a formative check and an assessment prompt. Add your own examples, timing and class-specific scaffolds.

Do the plans match a specific syllabus statement?

They are designed to connect broad concepts across Cambridge IGCSE, IB MYP and IB DP Mathematics: Analysis & Approaches. Check your current subject guide and school sequence when planning.

How can I use the assessment prompts?

Use the quick checks during learning to surface misconceptions, then adapt the assessment evidence as an exit ticket, discussion prompt or short written task.

Can I use the resources for free?

Yes. Browse and adapt the ideas without signing up or joining a mailing list.
